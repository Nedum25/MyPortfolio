01About

Asking why, not only what next.

I'm Chinedu Matthew Uzim, an AI researcher, data scientist and statistician based in Leeds. Most models are built to predict what will happen next. I'm interested in the harder question underneath: why it happens, and what would change if someone acted on it.

Alongside my PhD at Leeds Beckett University, I design and ship AI products. I built SmartSkan, Skannr's symptom-to-scan recommendation system, and UziIQ, a live review-analysis tool.

Earlier in my career, I worked with national survey, immunisation and electricity data at the National Bureau of Statistics, the National Primary Health Care Development Agency and Eko Electricity Distribution Company in Nigeria.

02Research

From prediction to cause and effect.

Doctoral research · Leeds Beckett University · 2025 – present

Causal AI for decisions in the real world

Most AI systems are good at prediction but say little about cause and effect, which is what decision-makers need when they ask what would happen if they intervened.

My PhD develops causal AI methods, with a focus on causal discovery: learning cause-and-effect structure from observational data that is short, noisy, incomplete or changing over time. Food security and climate in Sub-Saharan Africa is a key application, where stronger causal evidence could support earlier and better-targeted interventions.

Themes

  • Causal discovery from time series
  • Reliable methods under distribution shift and missing data
  • Moving from forecasts to interventions
